Electrical fires are a serious hazard that can cause significant damage to your home and put your family at risk. With proper awareness and a few preventative measures, you can greatly reduce that risk. Here's what every homeowner should know.

## 1. Schedule Regular Electrical Inspections

One of the most effective ways to prevent electrical fires is to schedule regular inspections with a licensed electrician. During an inspection, an electrician checks for potential issues like faulty wiring, an outdated electrical panel, or overloaded circuits, and recommends any necessary repairs or upgrades.

## 2. Avoid Overloading Circuits and Outlets

Overloading circuits is a common cause of electrical fires. Plugging too many devices, or too many high-wattage appliances, into a single outlet or power strip can cause the wiring to overheat.

**Tip:** Spread your electrical use across multiple outlets, and plug high-wattage appliances like heaters and air conditioners directly into wall outlets rather than power strips. If you're unsure whether your home's electrical capacity can handle your usage, consult a professional electrician.

## 3. Use Electrical Devices Safely

- **Appliances:** Always follow the manufacturer's instructions and never use damaged or frayed cords.
- **Extension cords:** Use them only as a temporary solution, never as a permanent fix. Don't run them under carpets or rugs where they can overheat, and avoid plugging high-wattage devices into them.
- **Power strips:** Choose ones with built-in circuit breakers that shut off power automatically if the strip becomes overloaded.

## 4. Replace Damaged Cords and Outlets

Damaged or frayed cords, and outlets that feel warm to the touch or emit a burning smell, are signs of an electrical hazard. Replace them immediately, and avoid using electrical tape as a long-term fix.

## 5. Consider Arc Fault Circuit Interrupters (AFCIs)

AFCIs are devices that detect electrical arcs, a common cause of fires, and automatically shut off the circuit when one is detected. Installing AFCIs adds an extra layer of protection to your home's electrical system.

## 6. Use Space Heaters Safely

Space heaters are one of the top causes of home fires if used carelessly. They draw a lot of electricity, and if left unattended or placed too close to flammable material, they can quickly become a hazard.

- **Placement:** Keep space heaters on a flat, solid surface at least three feet away from anything that can burn, including curtains, furniture, and bedding.
- **Power source:** Plug them directly into a wall outlet rather than an extension cord, which can overheat under the load.
- **Supervision:** Never leave a space heater running unattended, and turn it off when you leave the room or go to sleep. Look for models with automatic shut-off if they tip over or overheat.

## 7. Inspect Holiday Lighting

Holiday decorations bring a festive feel to the home, but old or damaged lights can pose a real fire risk. Frayed wires, broken bulbs, and overused extension cords can quickly turn into hazards. Before setting up holiday lights, inspect each strand for damage and replace any worn-out sets. LED lights run cooler and are more energy-efficient than traditional incandescent bulbs. Always turn off holiday lights when you leave the house or go to bed.

## 8. Install and Maintain Smoke Alarms

Smoke alarms are your first line of defense in detecting a fire early. Install them on every level of your home, inside bedrooms, and outside sleeping areas. Test them monthly, replace the batteries at least once a year, and replace the entire unit every 10 years.

## 9. Watch for Warning Signs of Faulty Wiring

Old or faulty wiring is one of the leading causes of electrical fires. If you live in a home more than 30 to 40 years old, your wiring may not be up to code or capable of handling modern electrical demands. Common warning signs include flickering lights, buzzing outlets, or a faint burning smell. If you notice any of these, schedule an inspection with a licensed electrician, who can assess your wiring and recommend upgrades.

## 10. Educate Your Family and Have an Emergency Plan

Make sure everyone in your household knows basic electrical safety and what to do in case of a fire. Develop an emergency plan that includes an evacuation route and a designated meeting point, and run through it together so everyone knows how to get out safely.
