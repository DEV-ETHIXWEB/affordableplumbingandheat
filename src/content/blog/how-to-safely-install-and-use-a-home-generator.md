When the power goes out, having a home generator can be a lifesaver. Whether it's due to severe weather, grid issues, or unexpected outages, a generator ensures your essential appliances and systems continue running. However, installing and using a generator requires careful planning to ensure safety for both your home and family. In this blog, we’ll walk you through key tips on safely installing and using a home generator.

## **1. Choose the Right Type of Generator for Your Needs**

There are two main types of home generators:

- **Portable Generators:** These are smaller units that can be moved around and manually connected to power specific appliances or circuits.
- **Standby Generators:** Permanently installed outside your home, these automatically start up during an outage and can power multiple systems, including HVAC, lights, and essential appliances.

When deciding which one to purchase, consider factors like the size of your home, the number of appliances you need to run, and your budget. Consult with a licensed electrician to determine the best option for your specific needs.

## **2. Install the Generator in a Safe Location**

Where you place your generator is crucial for safety. Here are some guidelines:

- **Keep It Outdoors:** Generators produce carbon monoxide (CO), a deadly gas that’s odorless and invisible. Never run a generator indoors, in a garage, or in any enclosed space.
- **Maintain Proper Distance:** Place the generator at least 15-20 feet away from your home, windows, and doors to prevent CO from entering your living space.
- **Stable Surface:** Ensure the generator is installed on a flat, dry surface. For standby generators, consider placing them on a concrete pad to provide a stable base and keep them secure.

## **3. Hire a Professional for Installation**

If you’re installing a standby generator, always hire a licensed electrician or a certified generator installer. Professional installation ensures that your generator is correctly connected to your home’s electrical system and meets all local codes and safety regulations. They’ll also install a transfer switch, which automatically disconnects your home from the utility grid when the generator is in use - this is vital to avoid backfeeding, which can be dangerous for utility workers.

For portable generators, have a professional install a transfer switch if you plan to connect the generator directly to your home’s circuits.

## **4. Understand How to Properly Operate Your Generator**

Before you experience an outage, familiarize yourself with your generator’s operation. Here are some best practices:

- **Read the Manual:** Every generator is different, so take the time to thoroughly read and understand the manufacturer’s instructions.
- **Regular Testing and Maintenance:** Periodically test your generator and run it for a few minutes to ensure it’s functioning correctly. Regular maintenance, such as changing the oil and checking fuel levels, will keep your generator ready when you need it.
- **Proper Fuel Storage:** Store fuel in approved containers, in a well-ventilated area, and away from living spaces. Ensure the generator is off and cool before refueling to avoid fire hazards.

## **5. Prioritize Safety Features**

When purchasing a generator, look for models that include safety features such as:

- **CO Detectors and Automatic Shutoff:** Some newer generators have built-in CO sensors that shut the unit off if dangerous gas levels are detected.
- **Overload Protection:** This prevents the generator from supplying more power than it can safely handle, reducing the risk of fire or electrical damage.
- **Weather-Resistant Enclosures:** If your generator will be exposed to the elements, ensure it’s properly protected with a durable, weather-resistant cover or enclosure.

## **6. Stay Prepared During an Outage**

When the power goes out and it’s time to fire up your generator, follow these key steps:

- **Disconnect from the Utility Grid:** If using a portable generator, make sure your main circuit breaker is turned off before connecting the generator to avoid backfeeding.
- **Limit Power Usage:** Avoid overloading your generator by only running essential appliances. A typical household will prioritize refrigerators, freezers, heating systems, and lights.
- **Monitor CO Levels:** Always have battery-operated CO detectors installed in your home when running a generator. These can alert you to dangerous levels of carbon monoxide.

A home generator can be a reliable backup during power outages, but it’s essential to prioritize safety from installation to operation. By choosing the right generator, placing it in a safe location, and following proper usage guidelines, you can ensure your home stays powered without compromising safety. When in doubt, always consult a professional for installation and maintenance advice.
