## Stay Calm and Assess the Situation

The first step when faced with a sewer backup is to remain calm and assess the extent of the problem. Take note of any visible signs of water or sewage backing up into your home, such as overflowing toilets or drains.

## Turn Off Water and Electricity

To prevent further damage, it's crucial to turn off the water supply to your home. Additionally, if the backup has affected electrical outlets or appliances, switch off the electricity at the breaker panel to reduce the risk of electrical hazards.

## Avoid Contact with Contaminated Water

Sewage backup contains harmful bacteria and pathogens, posing serious health risks. Avoid direct contact with contaminated water and sewage to prevent illness or infection. If you must handle sewage-contaminated items, wear protective gear such as gloves and boots.

## Document the Damage

Take photos or videos of the affected areas and any visible damage caused by the sewer backup. Documenting the extent of the damage can be valuable for insurance claims and reimbursement purposes.

## Clean and Disinfect Affected Areas

After the sewer backup has been resolved, thoroughly clean and disinfect the affected areas to eliminate bacteria and prevent mold growth. Use appropriate cleaning products and disinfectants recommended for sewage cleanup.

## Schedule Professional Cleanup and Restoration

Even after cleaning up visible signs of the sewer backup, lingering odors and hidden moisture may persist. Schedule professional cleanup and restoration services to ensure thorough remediation of the affected areas and prevent future issues.
