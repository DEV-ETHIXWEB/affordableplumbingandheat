When the frigid Colorado winter rolls in, a working furnace isn't just a luxury - it's a necessity. But what happens if your furnace suddenly stops working in the middle of the night, leaving your home uncomfortably cold? Knowing what to do in a heating emergency can make a big difference in both your comfort and safety. At Affordable Plumbing, Heat & Electrical, we understand how stressful this situation can be, so we've put together this guide to help you take the right steps when your heat goes out.

## 1. Stay Calm and Assess the Situation

Before you panic, take a moment to assess the problem. Is the furnace completely off, or is it running but not producing heat? Check your thermostat to make sure it's set to "heat" and that the temperature is set higher than the current room temperature. If it's programmable, check that the schedule hasn't been adjusted to a lower setting, and replace the batteries if it runs on them.

## 2. Check for Safety Issues First

If you smell gas or notice unusual sounds, like banging or rattling, from your furnace, safety should be your top priority. Turn off the furnace immediately and leave your home if necessary. These can be signs of a serious issue, like a gas leak or a cracked heat exchanger. In these cases, evacuate your home and call your gas company or emergency services right away. Never attempt to fix a gas-related issue on your own - it's a job for certified technicians.

## 3. Inspect the Furnace Power Supply

Your furnace needs a steady supply of power to operate. Check that it's plugged in, and if it's connected to a circuit breaker, confirm that the breaker hasn't tripped. If it has, reset it. However, if it continues to trip, there may be an underlying electrical issue, and you should contact a professional to have it safely addressed.

## 4. Check for Gas or Pilot Light Issues

If you have a gas furnace, the issue may be with the pilot light or gas supply. Ensure the gas valve is open, then check the pilot light - if it's out, follow the manufacturer's instructions to relight it. Be cautious: if you smell gas or hear hissing, there could be a gas leak. Leave your home immediately and contact your gas company or emergency services.

## 5. Replace or Clean the Furnace Filter

A clogged furnace filter is one of the most common causes of a heating breakdown. Dirty filters restrict airflow, causing the furnace to overheat and shut down. Check your filter and replace or clean it if it's dirty. Regular filter changes are one of the easiest ways to prevent breakdowns and keep your system running efficiently.

## 6. Look for Error Codes

Many modern furnaces have diagnostic lights that blink in specific patterns to indicate problems. Check your furnace's control panel for flashing lights, then consult your owner's manual to interpret the code. This information can help a technician diagnose the issue faster when you call for service.

## 7. Try Resetting the Furnace

Sometimes a simple reset can bring your furnace back to life. Many furnaces have a reset button, usually located near the blower motor. Press and hold it for about 30 seconds, then release. If the furnace starts running again, this may solve the issue temporarily, but it's still important to schedule an inspection to prevent future failures.

## 9. Stay Warm While Waiting for Repairs

While waiting for your furnace repair technician to arrive, it's important to stay warm. Wear extra layers of clothing, bundle up with blankets, and close off rooms you aren't using to conserve heat. If you use space heaters, keep them away from flammable materials. Also make sure you have a working carbon monoxide detector, since a faulty furnace can sometimes lead to dangerous gas leaks.

## Prevent Future Emergencies with Regular Maintenance

Once your furnace is up and running again, consider scheduling regular maintenance to prevent future emergencies. Annual tune-ups catch small issues before they turn into major problems, extend the life of your heating system, and help reduce your energy bills. In Colorado, where winter temperatures can drop significantly, taking care of your furnace year-round is essential for staying comfortable and avoiding unexpected breakdowns.
