If you own a home in Colorado Springs, you know the local climate and geography can present unique challenges for your plumbing system. From freezing winter temperatures to hard water problems, homeowners need to be vigilant about maintaining their plumbing to avoid costly repairs. In this post, we’ll explore the top five plumbing issues commonly seen in Colorado Springs homes and offer tips on how to prevent them.

## 1. Frozen Pipes

**Why It Happens:**  
Colorado Springs experiences frigid winters, and when temperatures drop, water inside exposed or poorly insulated pipes can freeze. Frozen water expands, increasing the risk of pipes bursting, which can cause extensive water damage to your home.

**How to Prevent It:**

- **Insulate Pipes:** Add insulation to pipes in unheated areas like basements, crawl spaces, garages, and exterior walls.
- **Let Faucets Drip:** During extremely cold nights, allow faucets to drip slightly to keep water flowing, reducing the risk of freezing.
- **Seal Leaks:** Make sure there are no air leaks around pipes where they enter your home, and close vents and openings that let in cold air.
- **Shut Off Exterior Faucets:** Drain and disconnect garden hoses, and shut off water to exterior spigots before the first freeze.

## 2. Hard Water Build-Up

**Why It Happens:**  
Colorado Springs has notoriously hard water, meaning it contains high levels of minerals like calcium and magnesium. Over time, these minerals can build up in pipes, fixtures, and appliances, causing clogs, reduced water flow, and even damage to your water heater.

**How to Prevent It:**

- **Install a Water Softener:** A water softener system can filter out the minerals that cause hard water, protecting your pipes and appliances.
- **Flush Your Water Heater Regularly:** Sediment can accumulate in your water heater due to hard water. Flushing it every 6–12 months can extend its lifespan and improve efficiency.
- **Use Vinegar to Clean Fixtures:** Soak faucet aerators and showerheads in vinegar to dissolve mineral deposits and improve water flow.

## 3. Leaky Faucets and Pipes

**Why It Happens:**  
Leaky faucets and pipes are common problems in older homes or homes with high water pressure. Leaks can occur due to worn-out washers, seals, or corroded pipes. Even a small leak can waste gallons of water and lead to higher water bills.

**How to Prevent It:**

- **Check for Signs of Leaks:** Regularly inspect under sinks, around toilets, and near your water heater for any signs of moisture or puddles.
- **Replace Worn-Out Parts:** Address dripping faucets by replacing worn washers or gaskets. Don’t ignore minor leaks, as they can worsen over time.
- **Monitor Water Pressure:** Excessive water pressure can put strain on your pipes and cause leaks. Consider installing a pressure regulator to keep water pressure within a safe range (typically between 40–60 psi).

## 4. Clogged Drains

**Why It Happens:**  
Clogged drains are typically caused by the buildup of grease, soap scum, hair, and other debris in the pipes. In Colorado Springs, tree roots can also invade underground pipes in search of water, leading to stubborn blockages.

**How to Prevent It:**

- **Use Drain Screens:** Install screens or filters in sinks and showers to catch hair, food particles, and debris before they enter the drain.
- **Avoid Pouring Grease Down the Drain:** Instead, dispose of cooking oils and grease in a container and throw it in the trash.
- **Schedule Regular Drain Cleaning:** Professional drain cleaning services can help clear out accumulated debris and prevent major blockages. Consider a camera inspection if you suspect tree roots are involved.

## 5. Water Heater Issues

**Why It Happens:**  
Due to the high mineral content in Colorado Springs' water, water heaters are especially prone to sediment buildup. This can lead to inefficient heating, strange noises, and a reduced lifespan for your unit. In addition, older water heaters may struggle to keep up with the demands of modern households.

**How to Prevent It:**

- **Annual Water Heater Maintenance:** Drain and flush your water heater once a year to remove sediment buildup. This can improve efficiency and extend the life of your system.
- **Adjust Temperature Settings:** Keeping your water heater’s temperature set between 120-140°F helps reduce the strain on the unit and prevents scalding.
- **Replace Old Water Heaters:** If your water heater is more than 10-15 years old, consider upgrading to a newer, more energy-efficient model.

Colorado Springs homeowners face unique plumbing challenges due to the region’s climate and water quality. However, with regular maintenance and a few preventative measures, you can avoid costly repairs and keep your plumbing system in top shape.
