Electricity powers our homes and lives, but it’s important to remember that it can also pose serious risks if not handled properly. For families in Colorado, where weather conditions and energy needs can vary, staying mindful of electrical safety is essential. Here are some practical tips to keep your home safe and your loved ones protected.

## **1. Inspect Your Electrical System Regularly**

Over time, wear and tear can affect your home’s electrical system. Regular inspections can help identify potential hazards before they become serious problems.

- Look for frayed wires, loose outlets, or scorch marks around switches and outlets.
- Schedule a professional electrical inspection, especially if your home is older or you’ve recently purchased it.
- Ensure all outlets and switches are functioning properly.

## **2. Childproof Your Home**

For families with young children, childproofing your electrical system is a must to prevent accidental shocks or burns.

- Install tamper-resistant outlets or use outlet covers to block access.
- Keep cords and wires out of reach.
- Teach children about the dangers of electricity and why they shouldn’t play with outlets or appliances.

## **3. Avoid Overloading Circuits**

Plugging too many devices into a single outlet or circuit can cause overheating and increase the risk of fire.

- Use power strips with built-in circuit breakers for multiple devices.
- Avoid plugging high-wattage appliances, like space heaters or air conditioners, into the same outlet.
- Distribute electrical loads across different outlets and circuits.

## **4. Practice Safe Cord Use**

Cords can be a hidden danger in your home if not used or maintained properly.

- Avoid running cords under rugs or furniture, as this can cause overheating.
- Replace damaged or frayed cords immediately.
- Use the correct extension cords for your needs, ensuring they’re rated for the intended load and are in good condition.

## **5. Be Cautious with Water and Electricity**

Water and electricity don’t mix. Always take precautions to prevent dangerous interactions.

- Keep electrical appliances and devices away from sinks, bathtubs, and other water sources.
- Dry your hands before plugging in or unplugging devices.
- Install ground fault circuit interrupters (GFCIs) in areas prone to moisture, such as bathrooms, kitchens, and outdoor spaces.

## **6. Prepare for Power Surges**

Colorado’s weather, including frequent thunderstorms, can lead to power surges that damage your appliances and electronics.

- Use surge protectors to safeguard sensitive devices like computers and televisions.
- Consider installing a whole-house surge protection system for added security.
- Unplug devices during severe storms to minimize risk.

## **7. Educate Your Family**

Knowledge is one of the best tools for ensuring electrical safety.

- Teach your family how to safely unplug appliances by pulling on the plug, not the cord.
- Show older children how to safely reset circuit breakers.
- Discuss the dangers of touching electrical equipment with wet hands or using devices near water.

## **8. Be Mindful of Outdoor Electrical Safety**

Colorado’s outdoor living spaces often feature electrical components like lighting, outlets, and appliances.

- Use outdoor-rated extension cords and devices.
- Keep outdoor outlets covered and protected from the elements.
- Stay clear of power lines and ensure your kids understand the importance of doing the same.

### **When to Call a Professional**

If you notice frequent electrical issues, such as flickering lights, tripped breakers, or outlets that feel warm to the touch, it’s time to call a licensed electrician. Attempting DIY electrical work can be dangerous and may lead to more significant problems down the road.
