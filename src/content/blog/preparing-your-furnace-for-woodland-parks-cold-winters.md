As the temperatures begin to drop in Woodland Park, Colorado, ensuring your furnace is ready to handle the winter chill is essential. Living in a mountain town means we experience some of the harshest winters, and your heating system needs to be up to the task. At Affordable Plumbing, Heat & Electrical, we know how to help you prepare your furnace for reliable and efficient performance during the cold months. Follow these tips to keep your home cozy and safe all winter long.

## 1. Schedule a Professional Furnace Tune-Up

A furnace tune-up is the most effective way to prepare your system for winter. A professional technician will inspect, clean, and service your furnace, ensuring it’s in top condition. Regular maintenance can identify potential issues before they lead to costly repairs or breakdowns during a snowstorm.

Our skilled team at Affordable Plumbing, Heat & Electrical performs thorough tune-ups to maximize your furnace’s efficiency and lifespan.

## 2. Replace or Clean Your Air Filters

Dirty air filters restrict airflow, making your furnace work harder and less efficiently. This not only increases your energy bills but also reduces your home's comfort. Check your furnace filter every month and replace it at least every 90 days—or more often if you have pets or allergies.

A clean filter keeps your furnace running smoothly and helps improve indoor air quality, which is especially important in Colorado’s dry winter climate.

## 3. Test Your Thermostat

Before winter hits, test your thermostat to make sure it’s functioning properly. A malfunctioning thermostat can cause uneven heating or prevent your furnace from operating altogether. Consider upgrading to a programmable or smart thermostat for more precise control over your home's temperature.

Programmable thermostats can help you save energy by automatically lowering the heat when you’re asleep or away, then warming the house just before you return.

## 4. Check and Seal Ductwork

Leaky or poorly insulated ducts can waste up to 30% of the warm air your furnace produces. Inspect your ductwork for visible holes, gaps, or disconnected sections. Use duct tape or mastic sealant to address minor issues, but for significant problems, it’s best to call in a professional.

Sealing ducts not only improves your home’s comfort but also helps reduce energy costs.

## 5. Clear the Area Around Your Furnace

Ensure there’s nothing blocking your furnace, such as storage boxes, furniture, or debris. A clear area allows for proper airflow and reduces fire hazards.

Also, check the furnace vent outside your home to ensure it’s free of obstructions like leaves, snow, or ice. Proper ventilation is crucial for your furnace to operate safely and efficiently.

## 6. Test Your Carbon Monoxide Detectors

Furnaces that burn natural gas or propane produce carbon monoxide, a colorless and odorless gas that can be deadly. While modern furnaces are designed to vent this gas safely, it’s important to have functioning carbon monoxide detectors in your home as a precaution.

Test your detectors regularly and replace batteries as needed to ensure your family stays safe throughout the winter.

## 7. Consider Upgrading an Old Furnace

If your furnace is more than 15 years old, it might be time for an upgrade. Newer models are far more energy-efficient, which can lower your utility bills and reduce your carbon footprint. Additionally, a new furnace provides peace of mind knowing your home will stay warm and comfortable no matter how cold it gets outside.
