Colorado winters are known for their cold temperatures and heavy snowfall, which can put a lot of stress on your home's plumbing system. If you’re a homeowner in Colorado Springs, preparing your pipes for winter is essential to avoid costly repairs from frozen or burst pipes. With a few proactive steps, you can protect your plumbing and keep your home warm and safe all winter long.

Here’s a step-by-step guide on how to prepare your pipes for Colorado winters:

## 1. Insulate Exposed Pipes

One of the most important steps you can take is insulating any exposed pipes. Pipes in unheated areas like basements, attics, garages, and crawl spaces are especially vulnerable to freezing. Use foam pipe insulation or pipe sleeves to cover these pipes, ensuring there’s a tight fit with no gaps. For extreme cold, consider wrapping pipes with heat tape, which adds an extra layer of protection by providing a controlled source of heat.

## 2. Disconnect and Drain Outdoor Hoses

Leaving hoses connected to outdoor spigots during winter is a common cause of burst pipes. When temperatures drop, water inside the hose can freeze, expanding and causing pressure to build up in the pipes. To prevent this, disconnect all hoses and drain them completely before storing them for the winter. Make sure to shut off the water supply to outdoor faucets and drain any remaining water from the pipes.

## 3. Seal Gaps and Cracks

Even small drafts can cause pipes to freeze. Inspect your home’s exterior for any gaps, cracks, or holes around doors, windows, and the foundation where cold air could enter. Use caulk or spray foam to seal these openings, especially near areas where plumbing lines run through exterior walls. This simple step can go a long way in keeping your pipes safe from freezing temperatures.

## 4. Let Faucets Drip on Cold Nights

When temperatures are expected to drop significantly, allowing your faucets to drip slightly can prevent pipes from freezing. Running water doesn’t freeze as easily as still water. Even a small, steady stream can relieve pressure in the system and keep water flowing, which helps reduce the risk of freezing and bursting.

## 5. Open Cabinet Doors

In areas like kitchens and bathrooms, where pipes are often located in cabinets, it’s a good idea to open cabinet doors during cold snaps. This allows warm air from your home’s heating system to circulate around the pipes, helping to keep them warm. This tip is especially useful for sinks located along exterior walls.

## 6. Maintain a Consistent Indoor Temperature

Keeping your thermostat set to at least 55°F, even when you’re away, can prevent your pipes from freezing. While it might be tempting to lower the heat when you’re not home, maintaining a steady temperature is more cost-effective than repairing burst pipes. Make sure all areas of your home, including basements and crawl spaces, are adequately heated.

## 7. Winterize Your Sprinkler System

Don’t forget about your irrigation system! Before the first freeze, drain your sprinkler lines and shut off the water supply. Use an air compressor to blow out any remaining water in the pipes to ensure they’re completely empty. If you’re unsure how to do this, consider hiring a professional to help winterize your system.

## 8. Know How to Shut Off Your Water

In the event that a pipe does freeze and burst, knowing how to quickly shut off your home’s main water supply can save you from significant water damage. Locate your main water valve and make sure it’s easily accessible. It’s also wise to label it clearly so everyone in the household knows where it is and how to operate it in an emergency.

## 9. Schedule a Professional Plumbing Inspection

A professional plumbing inspection can help identify vulnerabilities in your system before winter arrives. A local plumber familiar with Colorado Springs’ winter conditions can check for leaks, weak spots, and areas where pipes may be at risk. They can also recommend additional winterization tips specific to your home’s layout and plumbing setup.

Preparing your pipes for Colorado winters is a vital part of home maintenance. By following these simple steps, you can avoid the headaches and expenses associated with frozen or burst pipes. Insulating exposed pipes, draining outdoor water lines, and keeping your home’s temperature steady are all effective ways to protect your plumbing during the harsh winter months.
